Executive Summary

CIO View

The US equity market is in a mild risk-on but duration-constrained regime: the source composite is 55.26/100 (Mild Risk-On), while the independently weighted CIO score is 48.5/100. The 25 September close brought genuine tactical relief—S&P 500 +0.51%, Nasdaq +0.48%, MOVE −8.20% to 96.0—but VIX then surged to 16.24 (+9.21%) by 28 Sep live data, and the 5.17% 10-year yield, 48.33% 50DMA breadth and narrow leadership still demand selective beta.

The portfolio stance is neutral broad beta, selective risk-on. Friday's equity gain with VIX subsequently surging to 16.24 by 28 Sep live data keeps hedging urgency elevated, yet flat HYG, weak longer-horizon credit flows and narrow breadth argue against adding gross exposure. Add risk through cash-generative Technology, Cybersecurity and Health Care; treat Energy as an inflation hedge rather than momentum exposure after oil moved. Fund these positions from Utilities, Real Estate, weaker cyclicals and long duration. A sustained 10Y move below 4.75% with breadth above 55% would justify more beta; a 10Y break above 5.50% or HYG breakdown warrants lower gross and index convexity.

Preferred Theme: Cybersecurity (theme score 59.54; official strength score 67.37) — CIBR shows strong flow z-scores (2.1/2.3), quiet accumulation and the lowest dispersion among the three leading technology themes, with 65/100 grouped-flow reliability. Crypto Infrastructure scores higher mechanically at 68.77, but remains a more volatile, beta-heavy expression. Best GICS Sector: Information Technology (65.64) with strong uptrend confirmation.

Positioning: mild overweight IT, Health Care, and Cybersecurity; underweight rate-sensitive defensives (Utilities, Real Estate). Duration exposure minimized. Net ST active weights sum to 0.0%.

What Matters Now

  1. Volatility relief is not duration relief: MOVE fell 8.20% to 96.0 and VIX then surged to 16.24 (+9.21% on 28 Sep live), but the 10Y remains 5.17%. The 2s10s curve is positive by roughly 36bp; the key constraint is the level and cause of long yields, not curve inversion.
  2. Inflation persistence: Official August CPI was 3.4% YoY and core CPI 2.4%; final-demand PPI was 5.4% YoY. Energy and pipeline pressure keep policy asymmetry hawkish despite softer core inflation.
  3. Breadth deterioration: Only 48.33% of S&P 500 constituents above 50DMA despite the index being near all-time highs. This is a concentration risk — the rally is narrower than it appears.
  4. Sector rotation intensity: Energy +17.48% 60D vs Utilities −11.75% 60D. The 60D sector dispersion of 29.23 points is "very high," signaling active factor rotation rather than broad risk-on.
  5. Credit is calm on the day, weak underneath: HYG slipped 0.04% and LQD rose 0.06%, while modeled 60-day flow z-scores remain −1.2 and −2.0. Credit is not confirming an imminent equity break, but it is not validating broad risk-taking either.

Stale data note: University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment is 44.8 as of May 2026 (stale — 4 months old). It is not a current trigger. Current positioning is driven by rate moves, breadth, flow z-scores, and commodity activity — not stale sentiment.

Macro View

Short-Term Macro View — 1–3 Months

Labor market (Aug 2026): Nonfarm 159,075K, unemployment 4.1% (stable), participation 61.6%. Initial claims 215K (trending down). The labor market is at or near maximum sustainable employment based on these indicators — we do not characterize it in dire terms without corroborating deterioration in real-time activity data.

Growth: Real GDP 24,270B (Q1 2026), industrial production 102.65 (May 2026). Advance retail sales 773.9B (Aug 2026). The consumer remains employed but sentiment measures are mixed — the May 2026 Michigan reading of 44.8 is stale (4 months old) and not a current trigger.

Inflation (Aug 2026): BLS reports headline CPI at 3.4% YoY and core CPI at 2.4%. Final-demand PPI was 5.4% YoY. Disinflation in core CPI is constructive, but energy and producer-price pressure keep the risk distribution asymmetric.

Policy: On 16 September the Federal Open Market Committee raised the target range by 25bp to 3.75–4.00%, citing elevated inflation while describing economic activity as solid. Balance-sheet and money-supply rows are stale through May/June; they inform the structural backdrop, not the tactical call.

Long-Term Macro View — 6–12 Months

The base case is slower but positive growth with incomplete disinflation. The portfolio consequence is a higher required return for long-duration assets and greater reliance on earnings delivery. A benign path requires core inflation to move toward 2.5% without a material labor break; the adverse path is renewed energy or term-premium pressure that keeps the long end above 5% and compresses equity multiples.

Global: China GDP growth 4.96% (2025), inflation 0.06% (deflation risk). Euro Area GDP 1.41%, inflation 2.47%. Japan GDP 1.19%, inflation 3.17%. Divergent global cycles — Japan tightening, China easing, ECB on hold.
