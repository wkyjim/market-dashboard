Historical Analogs and Lessons

1994 rate-shock analog: the mechanism is rate-of-change, not absolute yield level. In 1994 the Fed doubled rates in 12 months and the long end sold off on a surprise hiking cycle; the 10Y was materially higher than today's 4.86%. The comparable element is the speed of repricing and the duration damage to growth multiples, not the yield level itself. Critical difference: today's Fed is hiking into a slowing economy with 3.7% CPI, whereas 1994 featured stronger growth and lower starting inflation. Equity outcome in 1994: flat-to-negative S&P 500 with severe growth underperformance; the lesson for 2026 is to reduce duration and growth beta before the hiking cycle peaks, not after.

2011 euro-crisis analog: headline Michigan sentiment in Aug 2011 was ~55.8, not below 50; the expectations subcomponent was weaker than headline. The mechanism is a growth scare with intact corporate fundamentals; equities sold off 19% peak-to-trough but recovered within 6 months as earnings held. The comparable element today is the gap between weak sentiment (Michigan 48.1 in Sep 2026) and still-resilient labor (165K jobs, 4.1% unemployment). Critical difference: 2011 had a sovereign-debt contagion channel absent today; today's risk is domestic inflation persistence. Lesson: do not over-react to sentiment weakness when labor and earnings hold, but size positions for a 10-15% drawdown scenario.

2018 credit-led-equity analog: credit spreads widened before equities rolled over in Q4 2018, with HY spreads leading by roughly 2-3 months. Today HY spreads are tight at 3.32% OAS and IG at 0.91%, so credit is not yet confirming the equity risk-off. The comparable element is the potential for credit to catch up if the Fed overtightens. Critical difference: 2018 featured a Fed that pivoted quickly; today's Fed is more hawkish with inflation at 3.7%. Lesson: monitor HY spreads as the early-warning indicator; a move above 4.0% OAS would validate the bear case and warrant reducing risk.
