Executive Summary

CIO View

Live 23 September US futures are mixed: S&P 500 futures at 7,821.75 (−0.13%) and Nasdaq 100 futures at 30,956.50 (−0.23%), versus the 22 September cash closes of 7,764.64 and 27,244.28 respectively. That calm overstates the opportunity set. The latest source breadth reading, dated 18 September, had only 45% of tracked constituents above their 50-day averages, with equal weight trailing cap weight by 3.51 percentage points over 20 sessions. The Fed raised its target to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September. This combination rewards profitable, resilient businesses but raises the hurdle for broad beta and indebted cyclicals. Our edge is selective exposure to cybersecurity and profitable technology, funded by weaker consumer, utilities and real estate exposures, with short Treasury bills as tactical liquidity. Energy remains an inflation hedge, though oil below $100 and the sector's strong prior run limit entry asymmetry. ETF flow estimates support cyber but disagree across close substitutes, so they are timing evidence rather than proof of institutional ownership. We would add broad risk only if breadth recovers and the long yield stabilizes; a simultaneous rate and credit shock would instead prompt lower gross exposure.

Historical Perspective

Prior rate shocks show that rising yields can compress equity multiples even when growth holds. They supply no reliable timing rule for this cycle. The current test is whether the long yield rises alongside earnings and broader participation, or alongside weaker credit and breadth. 1994 analog: defined by the rate-of-change/duration-shock mechanism, not absolute yield level — 1994 10Y was materially higher than 5%. 2011 analog: headline Michigan was ~55.8 in August 2011; it did NOT fall below 50. 2018 analog: credit-led-equity only as qualitative comparison unless lead time is independently sourced.

The Fed's 4.1% median end-2026 projection keeps a further increase plausible, but growth and inflation may alter that path. Our base case holds strategic broad beta neutral until either disinflation and breadth support a wider advance or credit and labor data confirm a slowdown.

Highest-Conviction Conclusions

  1. Duration constraint is real. The Fed at 3.75–4.00% with a 4.1% median end-2026 projection keeps the long end elevated. With the 10Y at 4.99% (23 Sep live), the term premium risk warrants underweighting long-duration assets.
  2. Narrow breadth demands selectivity. With only 45% of constituents above their 50DMA, broad beta is a low-conviction position. Edge comes from specific themes — cybersecurity, quality semis, energy — where flow and price confirmation align.
  3. Credit divergence is a watch signal, not a trigger. HYG and LQD persistent distribution diverge from equity stability. This is a caution signal; it does not yet confirm spread widening, but it removes a key confirmation pillar for risk-on.
  4. Stale sentiment data must not drive the stance. May Michigan at 44.8 is too old to confirm a current downturn. Current price, flow, and activity indicators drive the call; stale data only corroborates.
  5. Tactical cash is productive, not defensive. SGOV/SHY flow accumulation at 99/100 confidence offers dry powder for duration shock deployment. At 3.75–4.00% front-end rates, cash competes with risky assets on a risk-adjusted basis.
