Executive Summary (CIO View)

The US equity market is in a mild risk-on but duration-constrained regime: the source composite is 61.09/100 (Mild Risk-On), while the independently weighted CIO score is 48.5/100. The 25 September close brought genuine tactical relief—S&P 500 +0.51%, Nasdaq +0.48%, VIX down 5.11% to 14.87 and MOVE down 8.20% to 96.0—but it did not resolve the valuation constraint. The portfolio stance is neutral broad beta, selective risk-on. Friday's simultaneous equity gain and volatility compression reduces the urgency to hedge, yet flat HYG, weak longer-horizon credit flows and narrow breadth argue against adding gross exposure. Add risk through cash-generative Technology, Cybersecurity and Health Care; treat Energy as an inflation hedge rather than momentum exposure after oil moved. Fund these positions from Utilities, Real Estate, weaker cyclicals and long duration. A sustained 10Y move below 4.75% with breadth above 55% would justify more beta; a 10Y break above 5.50% or HYG breakdown warrants lower gross and index convexity.

Historical Analogs and Lessons

1994 Analog — Rate-of-Change / Duration Shock: The current environment resembles 1994 in terms of rate-of-change and duration shock — not absolute yield level. 1994 saw the 10Y rise from roughly 5.6% to 8.0% in a year. Current 10Y at 5.17% is materially lower in absolute terms. The comparable mechanism is the speed of repricing and the rise in bond volatility, not the level. Non-comparable part: absolute yield levels and the starting level of equity valuations differ significantly.
2011 Analog — Headline Sentiment vs Subcomponents: In August 2011, headline University of Michigan sentiment was ~55.8 — it did NOT fall below 50. Confusing headline with subcomponents (expectations, current conditions) can lead to erroneous conclusions. The current Michigan reading of 44.8 is from May 2026 and is stale — it should not be used as a current market trigger.
2018 Analog — Credit-Led Equity: The Q4 2018 correction was preceded by credit-market stress (HYG peaked in Nov, equity fell in Dec). We note this as a qualitative comparison only — the specific lead time between credit stress and equity drawdown is not independently sourced and should not be presented as a precise timing tool.
2022 Analog — Duration Repricing: The 2022 rate-hike cycle caused TLT to fall roughly 30% and SPX roughly 25%. The current 10Y move is sharp but the policy path is not comparable: the FOMC delivered one 25bp increase on 16 September 2026, taking the target range to 3.75–4.00%. Non-comparable: 2022 involved a sustained, multi-hike sequence; the present risk is a renewed inflation and term-premium shock after a single recent hike.

Macro View

Short-Term Macro View — 1–3 Months

Labor market (Aug 2026): Nonfarm 159,075K, unemployment 4.1% (stable), participation 61.6%. Initial claims 215K (trending down). The labor market is at or near maximum sustainable employment based on these indicators — we do not characterize it in dire terms without corroborating deterioration in real-time activity data.

Growth: Real GDP 24,270B (Q1 2026), industrial production 102.65 (May 2026). Advance retail sales 773.9B (Aug 2026). The consumer remains employed but sentiment measures are mixed — the May 2026 Michigan reading of 44.8 is stale and not a current signal.

Inflation (Aug 2026 unless noted): BLS reports headline CPI at 3.4% YoY and core CPI at 2.4%. Final-demand PPI was 5.4% YoY. Disinflation in core CPI is constructive, but energy and producer-price pressure keep the risk distribution asymmetric.

Policy: On 16 September the Federal Open Market Committee raised the target range by 25bp to 3.75–4.00%, citing elevated inflation while describing economic activity as solid. Balance-sheet and money-supply rows are stale through May/June; they inform the structural backdrop, not the tactical call.

Long-Term Macro View — 6–12 Months

The base case is slower but positive growth with incomplete disinflation. The portfolio consequence is a higher required return for long-duration assets and greater reliance on earnings delivery. A benign path requires core inflation to move toward 2.5% without a material labor break; the adverse path is renewed energy or term-premium pressure that keeps the long end above 5% and compresses equity multiples.

Global: China GDP growth 4.96% (2025), inflation 0.06% (deflation risk). Euro Area GDP 1.41%, inflation 2.47%. Japan GDP 1.19%, inflation 3.17%. Divergent global cycles — Japan tightening, China easing, ECB on hold.
