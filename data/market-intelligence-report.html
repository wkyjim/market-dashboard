Executive Summary

CIO View

We are in a mild risk-on regime with acute duration constraints. The S&P 500 trades near 7,650 with VIX at 14.8 — superficially benign. But the composition tells a different story: mega-cap tech and cybersecurity are carrying indices while 66% of S&P constituents sit above their 200DMA but only 45% above their 50DMA, signalling narrowing participation and deteriorating short-term momentum. The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% on Sep 16 — its first increase since July 2023 — and the dot plot signals one more hike possible this year. Yet the equity market is treating the hike as priced and the cycle as over. The 10Y Treasury at 5.00% and the 30Y at 5.33% disagree. Consumer sentiment at 44.8 — a low reading on data dated May 2026 (stale) — points to household stress even as index prints hold; treat as directional rather than confirming evidence. We would add risk selectively in cybersecurity, energy, and quality-growth semiconductors while hedging duration via underweight long-duration equities and overweights in cash/short-duration Treasuries. The most important question for the next quarter is whether the long end stabilizes or term premium accelerates — that single variable determines whether this is a soft-landing extension or a late-cycle top.

Historical Analogs and Lessons

Analog 1 — 1994 rate shock (duration). A rapid back-up in long-end yields compressed equity multiples even as the economy grew — a rate-of-change / duration-shock mechanism, not an absolute yield-level comparison (1994 10Y yields were materially higher than 5%). The lesson: term-premium expansion is not the same as a recession signal, but it is a multiple-compression signal. Current 10Y at 5.00% and 30Y at 5.33% resembles this regime.

Analog 2 — 2018 Q4 (credit leading equity). Credit spreads widened in advance of the Q4 2018 equity drawdown (claimed lead time and spread behavior not independently sourced here). HYG/LQD selling at −1.7Z/−1.9Z today qualitatively resembles early-2018 credit distribution; the comparison is limited and unverified for exact lead time. The equity market is currently ignoring this signal, as it did in early 2018. Confirmation requires HY OAS above 350bp.

Analog 3 — 2011 debt-ceiling (consumer sentiment decline). Headline Michigan sentiment was ~55.8 in August 2011 (expectations subcomponents fell below 50) while the S&P 500 held roughly flat, then declined once earnings confirmed the demand hit. Stale Michigan sentiment (44.8, May 2026) is far below the 2011 headline level; the analogy is limited to the direction of sentiment decline, not the absolute reading. Current retail sales remain strong, so the analog is incomplete.

The secular question is whether 3.75–4.00% is the terminal rate or merely the midpoint of a new tightening cycle. With fiscal deficits expanding, term premium structurally higher, and inflation sticky at 3.7%+, we lean toward higher-for-longer with a possible one-more-hike. The base case is a policy plateau at 4.00–4.25% through year-end, with the next directional move dependent on whether core CPI breaks below 2.5% or unemployment rises above 4.5%.
