Historical Analogs and Lessons

1994 (rate-of-change shock): the mechanism is the speed of tightening, not the absolute yield level (1994 10Y was materially higher than today's 4.93%). Today's 10Y near 4.93% with a rapid re-pricing of the long end is comparable in rate-of-change terms; duration assets are the casualty. Non-comparable: today's inflation is supply-driven (energy +20.4% YoY) rather than demand-driven, and the Fed is nearer terminal.

2011 (growth scare): headline Michigan sentiment was ~55.8 in August 2011, not below 50; the expectations subcomponent was the weak link. Today's Michigan 48.1 (September 2026, stale) is weaker in level but corroborating only - current price/flow evidence drives the stance. Non-comparable: 2011 had a euro-area crisis; today has a Hormuz/oil shock.

2018 (credit-led equity): credit spreads (HY OAS 3.26%, IG 0.89%) are calm, so the 2018 analog of credit leading equity down is not active. Credit would need to widen 50bp+ to validate the bear case.
