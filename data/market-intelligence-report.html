Executive Summary

CIO View

The 23 September selloff tested the mild risk-on thesis. The S&P 500 fell 0.76% to 7,706.03, the Nasdaq fell 0.69% to 26,936.04 and the Russell 2000 fell 1.28% to 2,838.66, while the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.11%. WTI crude fell 1.99% to $92.71, gold fell 1.23% and high-yield and investment-grade ETF proxies fell 0.74% and 1.14%. That combination is a discount-rate and financial-conditions warning, not a clean growth or safety trade. Breadth remains narrow at 45.0% above the 50DMA versus 66.1% above the 200DMA. The Fed raised its target to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September. This favors profitable businesses with pricing power but raises the hurdle for broad beta and rate-sensitive cyclicals. Our edge is selective exposure to cybersecurity and profitable technology, funded by weaker consumer, utilities and real estate exposures, with short-duration Treasuries as tactical liquidity. Energy remains a useful inflation hedge, but oil below $95 limits near-term pricing power. We would add broad risk only if breadth recovers and the long yield stabilizes below 4.8%; a simultaneous rate and credit shock would instead prompt lower gross exposure.

Historical Perspective

Prior rate shocks show that rising yields can compress equity multiples even when growth holds. They supply no reliable timing rule for this cycle. The current test is whether the long yield rises alongside earnings and broader participation, or alongside weaker credit and breadth. 1994 analog: defined by the rate-of-change/duration-shock mechanism, not absolute yield level — 1994 10Y was materially higher than 5%. 2011 analog: headline Michigan was ~55.8 in August 2011; it did NOT fall below 50. 2018 analog: credit-led-equity only as qualitative comparison unless lead time is independently sourced.

Macro View

Policy and inflation: The Fed unanimously raised its target to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September. The median participant projected 4.1% for end-2026, an individual assessment rather than a binding plan. Official BLS August CPI was 3.4% headline and 2.4% core year on year, versus conflicting source rows near 3.7% and 2.75%. We use the official release. Final-demand PPI was 5.4% year on year, with energy a large contributor to the monthly move; direct CPI pass-through is uncertain.

Growth and labor: Official August payrolls rose 162,000 and unemployment held at 4.1%. August retail sales improved (Census: $773.9B advance, +1.2% MoM), while starts declined. June claims, May sentiment and 2025 global rows are stale and do not drive the tactical call.

Rates: The 10Y yield closed at 5.11% on 23 September (+3.02%), while the 2Y closed at 4.85%. The curve remains upward sloping from 2Y to 10Y. A rising long end with weaker credit proxies suggests a term-premium and financial-conditions shock rather than an unambiguously stronger growth signal.

Long-Term Macro View — 6–12 Months

The secular question is whether 3.75–4.00% is the terminal rate or merely the midpoint of a new tightening cycle. The Fed's 4.1% median end-2026 projection keeps a further increase plausible, but growth and inflation may alter that path. Our base case holds strategic broad beta neutral until either disinflation and breadth support a wider advance or credit and labor data confirm a slowdown.

Highest-Conviction Conclusions

  1. Duration constraint is real. The Fed at 3.75–4.00 with a 4.1% median end-2026 projection keeps the long end elevated. With the 10Y at 5.11% (23 Sep closed row), term-premium risk warrants underweighting long-duration assets.
  2. Narrow breadth demands selectivity. With only 45.0% of constituents above their 50DMA, broad beta is a low-conviction position. Edge comes from specific themes — cybersecurity, quality semis, energy — where flow and price confirmation align.
  3. Credit divergence is a watch signal, not a trigger. HYG and LQD persistent distribution diverge from equity stability. This is a caution signal; it does not yet confirm spread widening, but it removes a key confirmation pillar for risk-on.
  4. Stale sentiment data must not drive the stance. May Michigan at 44.8 is too old to confirm a current downturn. Current price, flow, and activity indicators drive the call; stale data only corroborates.
  5. Tactical cash is productive, not defensive. SGOV/SHY flow accumulation at 99/100 confidence offers dry powder for duration shock deployment. At 3.75–4.00% front-end rates, cash competes with risky assets on a risk-adjusted basis.
