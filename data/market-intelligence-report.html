Executive Summary

CIO View

The latest completed U.S. session, 18 September, left the S&P 500 at 7,650.5, with VIX at 14.81. That calm overstates the opportunity set. Nasdaq gained while Russell 2000 fell; only 45% of tracked constituents are above their 50-day averages, and equal weight trails cap weight by 3.51 percentage points over 20 sessions. The Fed raised its target to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September, while the 10-year yield closed at 5.00%. This combination rewards profitable, resilient businesses but raises the hurdle for broad beta and indebted cyclicals. Our edge is selective exposure to cybersecurity and profitable technology, funded by weaker consumer, utilities and real estate exposures, with short Treasury bills as tactical liquidity. Energy offers an inflation hedge, though its strong prior run limits entry asymmetry. ETF flow estimates support cyber but disagree across close substitutes, so they are timing evidence rather than proof of institutional ownership. We would add broad risk only if breadth recovers and the long yield stabilizes; a simultaneous rate and credit shock would instead prompt lower gross exposure.

Historical Perspective

Prior rate shocks show that rising yields can compress equity multiples even when growth holds. They supply no reliable timing rule for this cycle. The current test is whether the long yield rises alongside earnings and broader participation, or alongside weaker credit and breadth.

Macro View

Short-Term Macro View — 1–3 Months

Policy and inflation: The Fed unanimously raised its target to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September. The median participant projected 4.1% for end-2026, an individual assessment rather than a binding plan. Official BLS August CPI was 3.4% headline and 2.4% core year on year, versus conflicting source rows near 3.7% and 2.75%. We use the official release. Final-demand PPI was 5.4% year on year, with energy a large contributor to the monthly move; direct CPI pass-through is uncertain.

Growth and labor: Official August payrolls rose 162,000 and unemployment held at 4.1%. The source's August retail sales improved, while starts declined. June claims, May sentiment and 2025 global rows are stale and do not drive the tactical call.

Rates: The source and API show the 18 September close at 4.75% for two years, 5.00% for ten years and 5.33% for thirty years. A higher long end amid stable credit would suggest nominal growth; a higher long end with weaker credit and breadth would tighten financing and compress valuations. The latter is the risk to hedge.

Long-Term Macro View — 6–12 Months

The secular question is whether 3.75–4.00% is the terminal rate or merely the midpoint of a new tightening cycle. The Fed's 4.1% median end-2026 projection keeps a further increase plausible, but growth and inflation may alter that path. Our base case holds strategic broad beta neutral until either disinflation and breadth support a wider advance or credit and labor data confirm a slowdown.
