We hold a mildly defensive net equity stance with quality and value tilts, trimmed tech exposure, and a preference for expressing views through relative value rather than outright beta.

The stance changes if (a) core PCE re-accelerates above 3.5% forcing a hawkish repricing, (b) HY spreads break above 4.0% signalling credit stress, or (c) the 30 October AI earnings reset reverses with a clean beat-and-raise from a hyperscaler. Until then, the base case is a choppy, range-bound market with sector dispersion doing the work.

Historical Analogs and Lessons

1994 analog: the mechanism is a rate-of-change and duration shock, not the absolute level of yields. The 1994 10Y was materially higher than today's 4.92%, so the comparison is about how fast the long end repriced and the stress on rate-sensitive assets, not about 5% being a trigger. Critical difference: 1994 had no AI-capex cycle and no megacap concentration, so the equity damage today is more concentrated in a single factor.

2011 analog: the Michigan sentiment headline was roughly 55.8 in August 2011 and did not fall below 50; the expectations subcomponent was weaker. Today's 48.1 September reading is genuinely lower, but it is stale (September observation) and a single sentiment measure is never the primary driver of stance. Use it as corroborating context only.

2018 analog: credit-led-equity is a qualitative comparison only; the lead time of credit over equity is not independently sourced here, so no timing claim is made. The relevant similarity is late-cycle factor rotation out of growth and into value under a hawkish Fed.
