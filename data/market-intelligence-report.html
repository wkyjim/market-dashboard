Macro View

Short-Term Macro View

Growth is cooling, inflation is still above comfort, and the next repricing hinges on whether the long end reflects stronger nominal growth or fiscal/inflation risk. The portfolio consequence is to avoid treating a lower front end as automatic permission to add duration.

Long-Term Macro View

The constructive case is productivity-led expansion; the adverse case is supply-driven inflation and a permanently higher term premium. Financials and Industrials benefit from the former, while Real Estate, Utilities and long-duration growth are most exposed to the latter.

Cross-Asset View

Equities still imply a soft landing, but gold strength and elevated long yields are a contradiction rather than confirmation. Credit has not yet delivered a systemic warning, which argues against a full risk-off posture; the signal becomes materially more negative if high yield breaks with equities. The dollar is a secondary constraint, while oil remains an inflation and geopolitical hedge.

Price Action, Breadth and Momentum

The prior dashboard showed broad participation—80.0% above the 50-day average and 74.58% above the 200-day average—but the latest weekly decline requires confirmation. The thesis is intact only while breadth remains above the prior correction lows and equal-weight does not materially underperform cap-weight.

Flows, Positioning and Sentiment

Modeled ETF flows are confirmation-only, not a basis for high-conviction sizing. Earlier evidence favoured Health Care, Financials and selective Industrials, while duration and Utilities were weaker. Treat the correction as a crowding reset, not proof of capitulation.

Historical Analogs and Lessons

The useful analogy is not a single year but a recurring pattern: strong index trends with rising term premium and narrow earnings leadership. The lesson is to keep exposure but demand better breadth and cash-flow support; the analogy fails if productivity-driven earnings broaden faster than rates rise.
