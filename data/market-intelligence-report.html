Historical Analogs and Lessons

1994 rate-shock analog: the mechanism is rate-of-change and duration shock, not absolute yield level. In 1994 the 10Y was materially higher than today's 4.91, so the comparable is the speed of repricing, not the level. The lesson: equity duration compresses when yields rise fast, and the damage concentrates in long-duration growth.

2011 Euro crisis analog: headline Michigan sentiment was about 55.8 in August 2011 and did not fall below 50; expectations subcomponents were weaker. Today's Michigan 48.1 (September) is far lower, so the analog holds only on the rates-volatility channel, not on consumer sentiment magnitude.

2018 credit-led-equity analog: qualitative only. Credit spreads widened before equity drawdowns, but lead times are not independently sourced, so we treat credit calm (IG 0.89, HY 3.26) as a near-term support, not a guarantee.
