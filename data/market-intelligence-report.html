CIO View

The market is constructive on nominal growth but less forgiving of duration risk. Friday’s broad rebound came after a volatile week in which the S&P 500 fell 1.4%, Nasdaq fell 2.1% and Russell 2000 fell 1.6%. That combination argues for a measured beta budget, not a wholesale risk-off stance: use quality, financials, health care and selective industrials, funded from long-duration and rate-sensitive exposures.

Short-Term View — 1–3M

Neutral beta with tactical OW in quality cyclicals and explicit index convexity. July CPI at 3.4% y/y and Q2 GDP at a 1.5% annualized advance rate leave the Fed data-dependent. Confirmation requires stable credit, breadth above 60% and a 10-year yield below the recent stress zone. Confidence 6/10.

Long-Term View — 6–12M

Selective overweight if earnings broaden and real rates stabilize. The base case is slower expansion rather than immediate recession, but expected return should come from EPS and free cash flow rather than multiple expansion. Confidence 6/10.

Macro View

Short-Term Macro View

Growth is slowing but not yet recessionary: BEA’s advance Q2 real GDP estimate was 1.5% annualized. Inflation is not cleared: July CPI rose 0.1% m/m and 3.4% y/y, with shelter contributing roughly two-thirds of the monthly increase while energy fell 1.5%. The equity transmission is asymmetric: softer data help only if inflation allows policy relief; otherwise they weaken earnings without lowering the long end. Confidence 7/10.

Long-Term Macro View

The 6–12M base case is a slow nominal-growth regime with policy constrained by above-target inflation and fiscal/term-premium sensitivity. Confirmation requires stable labor income, positive productivity/capex and contained credit spreads. China, Europe and Japan structural fields in the source are stale and excluded from high-conviction conclusions.

Price Action, Breadth and Momentum

Price action remains above the longer trend in the source, but the newest external close shows a failed attempt to extend the prior high: SPX and Nasdaq both posted weekly losses while small caps underperformed for the week. The correct interpretation is “trend intact, momentum less reliable,” not a confirmed bear reversal. Breadth should remain above 60% above key moving averages; below that threshold with VIX above 22 is the invalidation for the constructive base.
