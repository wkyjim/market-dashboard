Executive Summary

CIO View

We are in a mild risk-on regime with acute duration constraints. The S&P 500 trades near 7,650 with VIX at 14.8 — superficially benign. But the composition tells a different story: mega-cap tech and cybersecurity are carrying indices while 66% of S&P constituents sit above their 200DMA but only 45% above their 50DMA, signalling narrowing participation and deteriorating short-term momentum. The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% on Sep 16 — its first increase since July 2023 — and the dot plot signals one more hike possible this year. Yet the equity market is treating the hike as priced and the cycle as over. The 10Y Treasury at 5.00% and the 30Y at 5.33% disagree. Consumer sentiment at 44.8 — a multi-decade low — suggests the real economy is cracking even as index prints hold. We would add risk selectively in cybersecurity, energy, and quality-growth semiconductors while hedging duration via underweight long-duration equities and overweights in cash/short-duration Treasuries. The most important question for the next quarter is whether the long end stabilizes or term premium accelerates — that single variable determines whether this is a soft-landing extension or a late-cycle top.

Macro View

Short-Term Macro View — 1–3 Months

Fed policy: The Fed hiked 25bp on Sep 16 to 3.75–4.00%, its first increase since July 2023. The decision was unanimous with all 12 voting members. The dot plot shows 12 of 18 officials expecting one more hike this year. This is a hawkish surprise to a market that had been pricing the cycle as complete.

Inflation: CPI headline at 3.71% YoY (Aug), core CPI 2.76%, PPI final demand at 5.44% YoY — a worrying acceleration in producer-level prices that will eventually pass through.

Growth: GDP at 24,270 billion chained 2017 dollars (Q2 2026), industrial production stable, retail sales strong. The economy is not in recession yet, but forward indicators — Michigan sentiment at 44.8, housing starts declining — are deteriorating.

Labor: Unemployment at 4.1% (stable), initial claims at 215k (firming), continuing claims rising to 1.821M. The labor market is at full employment with no slack, limiting the Fed's ability to cut without inflation re-acceleration.

Rates: 10Y at 5.00% (+1.03%), 30Y at 5.33%, 2Y at 4.75% (+1.36%). The curve is inverted-to-flat at the front end but steepening at the long end — a term-premium signal. MOVE at 80.64 (+5.79%) confirms rising bond volatility.

Confidence: Moderate-High — data is fresh (Aug CPI/PPI, Sep FOMC), multiple sources agree on direction.

Long-Term Macro View — 6–12 Months

The secular question is whether 3.75–4.00% is the terminal rate or merely the midpoint of a new tightening cycle. With fiscal deficits expanding, term premium structurally higher, and inflation sticky at 3.7%+, we lean toward higher-for-longer with a possible one-more-hike. The base case is a policy plateau at 4.00–4.25% through year-end, with the next directional move dependent on whether core CPI breaks below 2.5% or unemployment rises above 4.5%.
