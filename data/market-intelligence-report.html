Historical Analogs and Lessons

1994 analog: the mechanism is rate-of-change, not absolute yield level. In 1994 the shock came from the speed of the Fed's tightening, not the level of the 10-year, which was materially higher than today's 4.93 percent. The comparable channel today is the speed of the long-end repricing driven by supply and inflation, not the level. The analogy fails if inflation cools and the curve bull-steepens.

2011 analog: headline Michigan sentiment in August 2011 was roughly 55.8, not below 50, so the current 48.1 reading (September 2026) is weaker than that episode. The comparable mechanism is a confidence shock feeding consumption; the non-comparable part is that 2011 featured a debt-ceiling crisis and European contagion absent today. Current sentiment is stale (September 2026) and corroborates rather than drives the stance.
